The consent layer for your face

Like robots.txt tells crawlers what they can index, me.txt tells the world what they can do with your likeness. Register once, enforce everywhere.

Why me.txt?

🔒

You Own Your Face

Your likeness is being used to train AI models, sell merchandise, and generate deepfakes — all without your permission. me.txt lets you declare exactly what is and isn't allowed.

📄

An Open Standard

me.txt is a simple, machine-readable text file anyone can serve. No walled gardens, no vendor lock-in. Platforms, AI pipelines, and content tools can all check a single endpoint for consent.

⚖

Enforcement-Ready

Every match generates an evidence packet with confidence scores, source URLs, and timestamps — ready for a DMCA takedown, a legal letter, or an automated cease-and-desist workflow.

How It Works

1

Register

Upload a few reference photos and set your consent rules — allow editorial, deny commercial, block AI training. Whatever you want.

2

Publish

Your profile gets a unique me.txt file at a public URL. Anyone can query it to check your permissions before using your likeness.

3

Detect

Our scanner uses face recognition to find your likeness on web pages. When it finds a match, it checks your consent rules and flags violations.

4

Enforce

Violations generate signed evidence packets you can send to platforms, legal teams, or automated takedown APIs.

Real-World Scenarios

BLOCKED

AI Training Dataset

A startup scrapes public photos to train a face-generation model. Their pipeline hits the /api/match endpoint, discovers your Deny: ai-training rule, and automatically excludes your images from the training set.

ALLOWED

News Article

A journalist uses your photo in a story about a public event. Their CMS checks your me.txt and sees Allow: editorial. The photo runs with proper attribution — no conflict, no takedown.

BLOCKED

Unauthorized Merch

Someone prints your face on t-shirts and lists them on Etsy. The Merch Monitor catches it, generates an evidence packet with a 96% confidence match, and you file a takedown the same day.

ALLOWED

Parody & Satire

A comedian uses a caricature of you in a sketch. Your Allow: satire rule signals that this use is fine. Both parties have clarity without a conversation.
